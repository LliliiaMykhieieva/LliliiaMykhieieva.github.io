2Market Customer Analysis.

Combining customer demographics, purchasing behaviour and advertising data to identify 2Market's highest-value customers, strongest product categories and most effective marketing opportunities.

Business Global Supermarket
Dataset 2,216 Customers
Focus Customer · Product · Marketing
Tools Excel · SQL · PostgreSQL · Tableau
01 / Business Problem

2Market had customer and campaign data — but needed to know where growth was actually coming from.

The objective was to translate customer, product and advertising data into clear commercial priorities for targeting, product strategy and marketing investment.

The analysis examined who 2Market's customers are, which age groups spend most, which products lead across markets, how customer value varies geographically and which advertising channels generate the strongest response.

The final goal was not simply to produce descriptive statistics, but to build a business intelligence view that management could use to compare markets and make marketing decisions.

Four business questions

Who are our most valuable customers?

What do they buy?

Where is value concentrated?

Which channels should receive marketing budget?

02 / Analytical Workflow

From raw customer data to an interactive decision-making dashboard.

01

Excel

Cleaned customer data, removed duplicates and unrealistic values, created age measures and used Pivot Tables for exploratory and descriptive analysis.

02

Data validation

Reviewed small or non-representative categories, treated extreme values carefully and standardised dates and numeric fields.

03

PostgreSQL & SQL

Structured customer and advertising tables, joined datasets and used aggregations to analyse products, customers and campaign performance.

04

Tableau

Built an interactive dashboard combining KPIs, demographic views, spending analysis, country performance and advertising results.

06 / High-Value Segment

Customers aged 50–59 are not simply the largest group — they also drive the highest category spending.

The 50–59 segment generated approximately $192K in Alcohol spending and $99K in Meat spending, leading the main product categories.

The same segment also showed the strongest response to advertising, making it particularly attractive for premium offers, personalised promotions and loyalty campaigns.

Commercial implication

Targeting should combine age, household context, spending behaviour and market — rather than relying on demographics alone.

09 / SQL Analysis

PostgreSQL connected customer behaviour with campaign performance.

Country analysis

Joined customer and advertising tables and aggregated conversions by country to identify the strongest platform in each market.

Customer segments

Compared advertising performance across marital-status groups to understand whether channel effectiveness changed by customer type.

Product + marketing view

Aggregated category spending and advertising activity together to investigate whether stronger marketing presence aligned with purchasing activity.

The SQL analysis showed Instagram leading in Australia, Canada, South Africa, Spain and the USA; Facebook leading in Germany; and no clear single leader in India.

10 / Dashboard

The final Tableau dashboard turned separate analyses into one management view.

Executive KPIs

Average age, strongest age segment, top product categories, largest market and strongest advertising channel provide an immediate performance summary.

Customer spending

Category spending by age group shows which customers are generating revenue and which products dominate their baskets.

Geographic comparison

Average spend and response metrics allow markets to be compared on both customer value and marketing engagement.

Marketing performance

Channel-level conversion views make underperforming advertising visible and support budget reallocation.

Interactive filters allow users to explore the dashboard by country and customer characteristics, moving from an executive overview to more detailed analysis.

11 / Recommendations

Grow the strongest areas — while reducing concentration risk.

01

Focus on the core customer.

Target 50–59 year-old, financially stable households with personalised promotions, premium products and loyalty incentives.

02

Grow Alcohol strategically.

Use premiumisation, pricing strategies, loyalty programmes and recurring-purchase propositions to build on the strongest category.

03

Increase Meat basket value.

Introduce bundles and complementary cross-selling — for example meat with wine, vegetables or other high-affinity products.

04

Diversify the product mix.

Reduce reliance on Alcohol and Meat by testing offers across lower-spending categories and identifying opportunities for complementary purchasing.

05

Protect Spain, but reduce dependence.

Maintain strong inventory and marketing support in the largest market while building customer acquisition and engagement in other high-value countries.

06

Optimise by market.

Use premium strategies in the USA, Germany and Canada; optimisation in Spain and Australia; and more localised pricing and propositions in lower-value markets such as India.

07

Prioritise digital marketing.

Shift activity toward channels demonstrating stronger successful conversion volumes, particularly Instagram, while reducing reliance on low-volume brochure advertising.

08

Fix high-value, low-response markets.

Investigate why customers in markets such as the USA and Germany spend strongly but respond relatively weakly to campaigns, then test market-specific creative, offers and targeting.
